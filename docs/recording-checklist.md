## How to record

Use one voice for all 171, the narrator's. The game never speaks as Rick; it always calls him "he".

- **Voice:** a calm, warm, older storyteller. A lower voice with crisp consonants will be easier for Rick, since hearing loss with age takes the high sounds first.
- **Pace:** slow and unhurried, about 130 words a minute. A short pause at each line break, and a longer one at each blank line between stanzas.
- **Exactly as written:** each poem starts with its title and its poet, then the poem. Fragment numbers and editors' brackets are already left out, and the Bible and book references are written the way they are said.
- **Files:** MP3, named exactly as shown, all lowercase, all in one folder called voice. A little silence at each end is fine; I'll trim them and even out the volume.
- **Tick the box** beside each one as you go.

A prompt to paste in before each piece:

> Read the text below aloud exactly as written, in a calm, warm, older storyteller's voice, slowly and clearly. Pause briefly at each line break and longer at each blank line. Don't add or change any words.

If ChatGPT won't give you the recordings as files, OpenAI's text-to-speech can make all 171 in one go from the list that came with this script.

By William Cullen Bryant.

In the long way that I must tread alone,\

Twenty Faces.\
By Claude.

Plato built water out of these:\

offers a face, moves on,\
and wears the glass of broken bottles smooth\

By Claude.
